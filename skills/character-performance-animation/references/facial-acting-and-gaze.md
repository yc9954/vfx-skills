# Facial acting and gaze

Design the face as a coordinated transition, not a library label pasted onto the
body. Start with attention and thought, then choose the smallest readable combination
of eyes, lids, brows, cheeks, mouth and jaw allowed by the character design and rig.

## Gaze as an acting chain

Record `current target | trigger | eye move | head follow | body follow/hold | dwell |
release/next target`.

- Eyes often acquire a nearby target before the head; larger changes may recruit
  head and torso. Vary the delay by urgency, confidence, effort and anatomy.
- Use eye darts to show active search, recalculation or suppressed attention. Do not
  add continuous scanning to a character who has already committed.
- Keep pupil/iris direction readable in the camera and correct for stylized eye
  geometry when needed. Apparent convergence matters more than mathematically
  identical rotations.
- Shared glances need a cause and exchange: A looks to B, B receives/returns or
  deliberately misses it, and both redirect according to the relationship.
- Before object interaction, establish whether the character looks at the object,
  contact point, receiver or partner. Expertise may shorten but not erase all checks.

## Blink timing

Blinks can punctuate a thought change, reset after a gaze shift, protect an impact,
release tension or sit inside a hold. Avoid clockwork blinking.

- Design closure and reopening as an asymmetric event rather than a symmetric scale.
- A complete blink usually closes the lids; partial lids are a separate expression.
- Do not blink across the one frame where the audience must read a critical glance
  unless concealment or comic timing is intentional.
- Preserve character style and rig capability. Human measurements inform nuance but
  do not impose one blink curve on every creature.

## Head-eye coordination

Use one of four purposeful patterns:

- **eye lead, head follows** for ordinary attention shifts;
- **head and eyes nearly together** for urgent or large reorientation;
- **eyes hold while head/body move** to maintain social or task focus;
- **head leads, eyes arrive late** for reluctance, fatigue, concealment or a designed
  stylized take.

Name the reason. Avoid eyes sliding continuously while the head remains disconnected.

## Face as connected regions

- **Brows and upper lids** set alertness, pressure and attention; avoid both brows
  moving as a mirrored switch unless the expression calls for it.
- **Lower lids and cheeks** help connect effort, smile, suspicion or strain to the
  eyes. A mouth-only smile often feels pasted on.
- **Mouth corners, lips and jaw** define direction, compression, release and speech/
  effort. Jaw opening should belong to breath, sound or emotional pressure.
- **Nose/muzzle/cheek mass** may transmit mouth action into the eyes when the design
  supports it. Preserve firm landmarks that keep the face coherent.
- Keep emotion, speech and blink layers coordinated but distinguishable. A blink
  should not erase the expression; a phoneme should not become the whole emotion.

## Transitions and asymmetry

Animate the path between expressions. Decide which feature learns the news first,
where tension accumulates, what releases and what lingers. Use restrained asymmetry
to show uncertainty, private thought or personality; random left/right offsets create
noise. Avoid instant neutral-to-iconic expressions with every region arriving together.

## Micro-reactions

A micro-reaction is a brief, motivated change that the audience can perceive in the
shot: a small lid tighten, brow check, mouth-corner hesitation, jaw release, eye dart
or breath catch. It must answer a trigger. Choose one or two channels, give them room,
then let the primary beat continue. Do not stack micro-reactions until the face jitters.

## Scale and visibility

At miniature scale, lead with head direction and body silhouette. Add facial detail
only if the render resolution, contrast and angle can show it. Use close diagnostic
views to inspect the rig, but do not claim that hidden microacting improves the locked
master. If facial controls are absent, transfer the intent to head, shoulders, hands,
breath and timing without inventing capabilities.
