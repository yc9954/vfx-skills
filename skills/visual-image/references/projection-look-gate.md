# A cinematic look in a locked projection view

Use for a tabletop look-frame or lighting repair, not as a universal film style.
Receive the actual camera/mapping/plate anchors and performance focal action. Do
not tilt the camera, introduce shallow focus or stretch the raster for drama.
For image-first work, inspect the actual generated plate/character image in its
selected source view. Do not require a Blender scene, debug render or previs to
approve that image's composition and look. Final four-seat mapping is a later check.

## Define before rendering

Record `frame/version | fixed geometry | first read | second read | quiet region |
motivated light sources | palette/material separation | delivery-scale rejects`.

- First read is the story action, not necessarily the brightest or largest plate.
- Shape the character and contact with a localized key; reduce competing fill or
  background contrast. A lantern should affect nearby surfaces, not just glow as
  a flat icon. Shadow direction must obey the seat/mapping contract.
- Separate satin cloth, diffuse skin, glazed ceramic, aged metal and wet food by
  highlight character, not by assigning the same gloss to every object.
- Use a restrained warm practical/cool ambient relationship when it fits the
  reference. Do not bake that palette into every future scene.
- Preserve visible feet/hands/receiver in shadow. Crushed blacks, blanket bloom,
  exposure pumping and depth-of-field blur are not shortcuts to cinema.
- Reduce high-frequency ornament near a tiny actor. Frame the action with set
  structure and negative space; density alone does not make a palace.

## Checks at the selected stage

### Near-top-down camera check

For this series, interpret the requested top view as **75° elevation above the
horizontal floor, 15° away from vertical downward**. Use 90° only when the user
explicitly selects a perfectly vertical view. Bind plate, actors and props to
the same source camera; a top-down plate with frontal portrait actors fails.

Include observable projection cues in the image prompt:
For a replay of a user-preferred successful prompt, preserve its original wording,
scale constraints and reference roles instead of replacing it with the example
below. Apply only the user's requested changes; use this check on the actual result.

> Preserve the supplied plate image's camera, centre, diameter and rim outline.
> Look almost straight down from above: 75° above the floor, 15° from vertical.
> See the tops of braided heads, shoulders, forearms and tools. Standing bodies
> are foreshortened beneath the heads. Characters looking down at their task
> have mostly obscured faces. Entity references provide identity and costume,
> never their frontal portrait camera. Keep the nearly circular plate outline;
> do not restage the scene from an oblique or eye-level view.

Inspect the **actual image** before handing it to Kling:

- Compare the plate centre, diameter and rim proportions with the supplied plate
  image. New ellipses, scale changes and reframing are failures to preserve it.
- Verify that heads/shoulders and jug openings are seen from above, bodies are
  shortened in projection, and grips/tool paths remain readable. Dominant frontal
  faces, full-length front torsos or a low side silhouette fail this camera brief.
- Record source/target versions, visible evidence and any registration drift.
  Fix the failed image within the authorized scope before video generation.
  Do not send a known failed image to Kling as a prototype or expect Kling to
  repair its source perspective.
- Compare actor size relative to the plate against the user-preferred result and
  preserved scale instructions. Do not accept enlarged actors merely because
  their heads now read from above.

The angle written in a prompt does not measure the output camera. A near-circular
plate and overhead silhouettes support a visual judgment; they do not certify
an exact 75° camera or physical projector calibration.

### Look and readability

1. Inspect silhouettes, visible feet/hands and intended contact in the supplied
   or generated image. Use a separate contact/debug view only when needed; it is
   not a style target or evidence of motion correctness.
2. Compare the representative generated image or rendered style frame in the
   **same selected source/registered view**, with intended materials/light.
   Compare at full size and at intended display scale when known. Once four-seat
   composition exists, inspect each rotated tile and the shared center; the
   correct solution need not be a single global cinematic focal point.

Save a brief decision: accepted internally for the next selected step / failed / unverified,
with failed variables. A good still does not approve acting or contact. Do not
carry old checks to changed geometry or pretend a wider diagnostic is the master.

## Hand off the real result

Pass the actual generated image or rendered look frame with its input/view/version, not only
adjectives or an oblique mood image. If an oblique reference is also useful, label
it material/light only and explicitly exclude its lens, inventory and layout.
For a generative call, retain high-fidelity anchors/contact while allowing only
the declared appearance/performance freedoms. Current budget still governs calls.
