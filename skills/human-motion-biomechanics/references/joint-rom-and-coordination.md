# Joint ROM and whole-body coordination

Use this reference to decide how an action should distribute motion across the
body. It is an animation/design guide, not an individual range-of-motion test.

## Working range is not anatomical maximum

Treat range as four production zones rather than a universal degree table:

1. **Neutral/mid-range** — useful default under load or repetition.
2. **Task working range** — farther motion that remains coordinated and readable
   for this character, prop and duration.
3. **Near-limit** — requires an intentional reason, short exposure and visible
   compensation elsewhere in the chain; verify against reference.
4. **Exceeded/unknown** — target demands stretch, joint-axis error, skin collapse,
   pain acting not requested by the user, or an uncalibrated rig. Redesign or mark
   unverified instead of silently clamping.

An anatomical maximum measured in one plane does not imply that all axes can reach
their maxima simultaneously, under load, repeatedly, or with the same comfort.
Character proportions, clothing, load, fatigue, body history and intentional style
change the usable range. Store action-specific qualification, not “all joints pass.”

## Region map for animation

| Region | Separate in the motion plan | Near-limit compensation / common failure |
|---|---|---|
| Shoulder complex | Glenohumeral motion plus scapular/clavicular participation; arm elevation and rotation are not one upper-arm channel | Adjust elbow/forearm, shoulder girdle, trunk facing or feet; do not stretch the arm or collapse the armpit |
| Elbow | Flexion/extension, elbow direction and continuity | Reposition shoulder or prop; avoid locked extension under a changing load or a pole flip near straightening |
| Forearm | Pronation/supination distinct from wrist rotation | Rotate forearm and adjust elbow/shoulder; do not twist the palm around a fixed forearm using only the wrist |
| Wrist | Flexion/extension and radial/ulnar deviation; near-neutral is a strong default for forceful or sustained handling | Change handle angle, grip, elbow or body position; do not accept target reach with extreme wrist bend |
| Hand/thumb | MCP/PIP/DIP roles, thumb CMC opposition, palm arch and contact pads | Resize/reorient the grasp or use two hands; a single curl or upright thumb is not secure opposition |
| Hip/pelvis | Hip flexion/extension, ab/adduction and rotation plus pelvic translation/tilt | Step, widen/reorient stance or move the prop; do not solve a reach by folding only at the waist |
| Knee | Primary flexion/extension with continuous direction and load absorption | Share with hip/ankle and foot placement; avoid twist through a planted foot or snapping at extension |
| Ankle/foot | Dorsi/plantar flexion, inversion/eversion, heel/forefoot/toe support and roll | Replant or change stance; an ankle target on the floor does not prove sole support |
| Trunk | Lumbar/thoracic flexion, extension, lateral bend and rotation distributed by task | Turn or step before large loaded twist; do not put all rotation in one waist bone |
| Neck/head | Gaze, head, neck and upper trunk participation | Let torso share a large turn; do not use neck over-rotation to preserve a frozen body |

The table describes motion responsibilities, not mandatory bone counts or fixed
ratios. A stylized or simple rig can use approximations if the intended action,
silhouette, contact and deformation remain credible.

## Kinetic chain

Plan functional movement as task-specific sequencing across linked segments. For
loaded handling, a useful trace is:

`ground/support -> feet/ankles -> knees/hips -> pelvis/trunk -> shoulder girdle ->
elbow/forearm -> wrist/hand -> prop`

This is not a rule that every joint must visibly move. It asks where support and
load travel, which segments stabilize, which initiate, and which follow. A hand can
remain nearly still relative to a handle while the body reorganizes underneath it.

## Compensatory-motion ladder

When a distal joint approaches its usable range, test the smallest story-preserving
change first:

1. Reorient the prop, handle or contact patch.
2. Reassign roles between the two hands.
3. Reposition the elbow and distribute forearm rotation.
4. Add shoulder-girdle and trunk participation.
5. Rotate the pelvis, shift weight or step/replant the feet.
6. Change staging or prop placement if the action still requires an extreme pose.

Compensation is not permission for random sway. It must improve reach, support or
load control and remain consistent with the character and shot.

## Blocking questions

- Is the joint frame calibrated on the evaluated rig, or inferred from a name?
- Which joints are moving, stabilizing or near a limit in each phase?
- Does a legal endpoint hide a pole flip, interpenetration or illegal path between frames?
- Does compensation preserve established hand/foot contact and the intended action?
- Does the dressed mesh retain volume and avoid unacceptable body/clothing/prop overlap?
