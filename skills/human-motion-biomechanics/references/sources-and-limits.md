# Evidence sources and application limits

Checked 2026-09-30. These sources inform a production abstraction for animation.
They do not provide medical diagnosis, individualized safety limits, force closure
proof or a universal table of legal character poses.

## Anatomy and range of motion

- [OpenStax, Anatomy and Physiology 2e §9.6](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/9-6-anatomy-of-selected-synovial-joints):
  distinguishes joint structures and motions, including forearm rotation, elbow,
  knee, ankle and regional spinal behavior. Use to avoid an all-joints-are-free-XYZ
  rig model; it does not prescribe one animation rig or comfortable work range.
- [American Society for Surgery of the Hand, Upper Extremity Joints](https://www.assh.org/handcare/safety/joints):
  supports separating MCP/PIP/DIP, thumb joints and forearm rotation. Clinical
  anatomy is translated only into contact/axis questions, not diagnosis.
- [NASA Human Integration Design Handbook](https://www.nasa.gov/organizations/ochmo/human-integration-design-handbook/):
  documents ROM as a human-system design concern and notes whole-body/segment
  participation and population/context variability. Its spacecraft design data and
  measured values are not copied as universal character limits.
- [NIOSH manual-materials-handling topic](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/niosh/mining/topics/manualmaterialshandling.html):
  notes that joints work efficiently near mid-range and that force, repetition and
  awkward sustained posture interact. This supports distinguishing working range
  from maximum, not banning brief expressive poses.
- [OSHA neutral working positions](https://www.osha.gov/etools/computer-workstations/positions):
  describes neutral alignment as a comfortable work posture. It is workstation
  guidance, so use the neutral principle for sustained/loaded animation, not as a
  universal acting pose.

## Load, support and handling

- [CDC/NIOSH Revised Lifting Equation](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/ergonomics/about/rnle.html):
  identifies weight, horizontal/vertical hand location, travel, asymmetry, frequency,
  duration and coupling quality as relevant to two-handed lifts. The production layer
  uses these as qualitative variables; it does not calculate a safe load.
- [HSE good handling technique](https://www.hse.gov.uk/msd/manual-handling/good-handling-technique.htm):
  supports planning, stable stance, good hold, keeping a load close, moving feet
  instead of loaded twisting and smooth control. Workplace guidance is adapted to
  visible action beats, not a single mandatory lifting style.
- [NIOSH Practical Demonstrations of Ergonomic Principles](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/media/pdfs/2026/03/2011-191.pdf):
  supports neutral tool posture, power-grip handle fit and two-hand support for heavy
  tools. Its dimensions/force thresholds are not universalized to stylized hands.
- [CCOHS Pushing and Pulling — General](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/ergonomics/push1.html):
  emphasizes body weight/strength, force height/direction/distance, posture, friction,
  duration and travel. The source itself warns that no single directly applicable
  numerical standard covers all push/pull work; this skill therefore avoids one.
- [OpenStax College Physics §9.3 Stability](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics/pages/9-3-stability) and
  [§9.6 Forces and Torques in Muscles and Joints](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics/pages/9-6-forces-and-torques-in-muscles-and-joints):
  support base-of-support, center-of-gravity and lever-arm reasoning. Static examples
  are not a dynamic gait validator, and simplified mechanics are not internal-force proof.

## Coordination, posture and grasp

- [Yiou et al., Adaptability of anticipatory postural adjustments, 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3377909/):
  reviews postural adjustments that precede voluntary movement and their adaptation
  to constraints. This supports preparation before a load-changing action; it does
  not prescribe one visible pre-motion for every gesture.
- [Sciascia et al., role of the kinetic chain in shoulder rehabilitation, 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7202723/):
  defines kinetic chain as task-specific sequential activation and discusses trunk/
  lower-limb contribution. Much evidence is sports/rehabilitation based; use the
  coordination concept, not sport-specific percentages or injury claims, for cooking.
- [Feix et al., comprehensive GRASP taxonomy](https://www.csc.kth.se/grasp/taxonomyGRASP.pdf) and
  [2016 journal paper](https://www.eng.yale.edu/grablab/pubs/Feix_THMS2016.pdf):
  organize one-hand static grasps by power/precision and opposition. The taxonomy
  excludes or underrepresents gravity-dependent and two-hand dynamic support, so a
  jug/tray still needs task-specific load and timing analysis.

## Animation readability

- [The Science Behind Pixar — Animation](https://sciencebehindpixar.org/pipeline/animation):
  supports breaking an action into meaningful key poses. It is craft/process evidence,
  not biomechanics validation.
- [Animation Mentor — lifting a heavy object](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/how-to-animate-weight-and-force-lifting-heavy-object/):
  recommends readable anticipation, grip/lift/recovery beats, silhouette, weight
  shift and follow-through. This is professional craft guidance, not peer-reviewed
  ergonomics; use it for staging and miniature readability only.

## Application policy

1. Prefer qualitative relationships and calibrated reference over universal angles.
2. Record when a claim is sourced fact, production inference or project convention.
3. Preserve individual/body-design variability and assistive supports.
4. Separate static pose plausibility from continuous contact and dynamic verification.
5. If actual human safety or clinical limits matter, stop using this animation layer
   as authority and obtain qualified, task-specific assessment.
