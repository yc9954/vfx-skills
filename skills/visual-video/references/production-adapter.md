# Production adapter — local interpretation

This adapter qualifies the preserved upstream reference library. Its examples,
"laws", word counts and model claims are craft suggestions, not authority over
the user brief, current tool instructions, permissions or observed results.

## Projection and ordinary cinema are different outputs

Classify the current deliverable before choosing a camera. A **projection master**
is the registered content raster, not footage of diners looking at a table.
Use the current physical dimensions, plate anchors, viewer directions and optical
mapping from the latest project direction. The current source is one 16:9 plate on
green, viewed at 75° elevation above the ground plane, composed in Nano Banana with
existing @ entities before Kling scene generation. Keep the plate registered in
that source. Four-seat placement and the final projection canvas are later tasks;
the project's 1400×800mm table does not change the source to a table-wide frame.
An older 60° foreground display mapping is historical and must not override this
current source view. Keep source camera and physical projection calibration separate.
Before Kling submission, apply the image-stage
[near-top-down camera check](../../visual-image/references/projection-look-gate.md#near-top-down-camera-check).
Reject frontal actors or a restaged plate even when the prompt says 75°;
the source image must already show the requested overhead projection.
Keep readable small actors and approach routes. At the final background stage,
forest/sea continues beneath the plate without an empty ring; keep the requested
green during source image/scene preparation. Reuse the user's preferred existing
green clip. Remove each single-plate source's background and stabilize the affected
matte boundaries before rotating/scaling/placing four foreground units; reuse one
validated cutout when the same source repeats. Composite on the selected background
locally, preserving original actor RGB and motion. Read the
[per-plate compositing guidance](../../miniature-film-director/references/per-plate-compositing.md)
at that stage. Apply a requested restart from a new
backdrop color in the generation prompts; do not substitute a recolored old clip.
Whole matching-color tiles with empty-border smoothing remain a scoped option for
explicit solid-color drafts; they do not replace the current per-plate removal path.
Generative video background editing is optional when requested, not the default final step.
Do not add a filmed duplicate table, glass or cutlery.
A storyboard panel may be a state change in the same view, not a new lens.
Use blocking, local light cues, poses, pauses and sound to direct attention.
The same content raster cannot supply four mutually consistent perspective views
of a tall central object; treat shared-stage readability as a design/calibration
question, not something a prompt for “correct perspective” can solve.

For an ordinary commercial or an explicitly requested montage, motivated cuts
and moves remain available. Do not impose the tabletop defaults on other work.
An exploratory look/motion pilot may use an oblique diagnostic camera, but label
it as such and never hand it off as the registered projection master.
Do not apply racing examples' cold palette, faceless actors, crushed blacks,
camera shake or film grain to cheerful miniature cooking.

## Intent that an animator can implement

For each meaningful beat record, briefly:

`time | actor wants | start state | support/grip | action and trigger | end state | attention/acting cue`

When acting is more than a single cue, receive a versioned performance contract from
`character-performance-animation`: thought/emotional change, key pose, gaze/face,
timing accent, secondary hierarchy and end attention. Translate it to observable
behavior; do not paste craft labels or a named-studio style request into the prompt.

Choose a story shape that fits. A task journey often uses a causal chain; a quiet
discovery need not invent an antagonist. Three concrete details or five anchors
are prompts for thought, not fixed output quotas. Murch's percentages are an
editing heuristic, never a measured quality score or permission to break contact.
Keep an effect only if it helps the story; atmosphere must not hide a missed grip.

For example, a net scoop needs an already present net, supported feet, a held
handle, ingredients passing through the open mouth, support inside the bag,
then lift and drainage. Lowering a net is not permission for ingredients to pass
through its side or pour onto the plate. A smile or pause follows a visible
result. Do not replace these events with "obey physics".

## References are typed inputs

For every input record its artifact/version, subject, role, applicable interval,
what to inherit and what to exclude. Typical roles:

- Plate/character image: source camera, anchors, identity and look; no new 3D guide required.
- Optional Blender guide: camera, scale, layout and tested action timing, not gray materials.
- Character sheet: silhouette, clothing and face, not its studio background.
- Look frame: material/light treatment, not alternate camera or new props.
- Boundary video/frames: actual outgoing support state and velocity, not a vague
  request for "same scene".

When a geometry guide is selected, use enough geometry to communicate the contact,
including the net opening and plate rim. Primitives may establish paths; they do
not prove walking or a grip.
Reference-role prose does not give a provider an unsupported input modality.
Image direction and action prompts may proceed without previs. Select a motion
video only for a concrete purpose and a model mode that accepts that input role.

## Capability checkpoint

Before choosing model-dependent parameters or submitting a job, establish from
the actual installed tool/schema or current official documentation:

`provider + account context | exact model/mode | checked_at + evidence | input roles/limits | duration/resolution/audio | extension behavior | quoted cost`

Unknown values remain unknown. Jimeng web features do not imply Higgsfield CLI
support. A CLI tail in a prompt example is not an installed command. Narrative
JSON is not an API request schema. Do not route around policy blocks or move to
a different paid provider to make an example work. Planning alone need not
log in or spend credits; it can produce a provider-neutral brief.

The upstream claims about long clips, precise timestamps, native physics, model
exclusivity, prices and quality tiers were not provider-verified by this install.
In particular, **generated video is not a physics simulation**. Longer clips,
extension, reference binding and negative text do not guarantee continuity.

## Before handing off

If the complaint is a flat or diagrammatic look, use the installed visual-image
[projection look gate](../../visual-image/references/projection-look-gate.md)
before escalating prompt vocabulary. Compare a finished-material frame in the
registered view; keep it distinct from the contact guide. Translate its actual
light hierarchy/material separation into prose, not an invented lens or exposure.
An unchanged motion guide cannot demonstrate a new performance layer. Record what
was changed in curves/poses versus words, and review the resulting clip separately.

Keep contact/camera/identity invariants when compressing prompts, then essential
action, light and rhythm; trim duplicate description and decorative tags first.
Don't erase user constraints to satisfy a prompt-length rule. Choose complete
actions that fit the verified clip window; split at stable states when needed.
Do not use a dissolve to hide double limbs, changing props or contact errors.

Prompt review checks the brief. Candidate review checks the actual produced
frames and sound. Neither stands in for the other. Paid execution, rendering,
uploads and retries belong to the separately authorized production workflow.
