# Grasp, grip security and tool use

Use when fingers, thumbs, handles, vessels or utensils matter. A grasp taxonomy
helps choose contact roles; it does not prove force closure, comfort or dynamic safety.

## Choose the grasp by task

Describe at least these dimensions:

- **Purpose:** support, carry, stabilize, steer, tip, resist torque, operate or place.
- **Force/precision:** power, intermediate or precision demand.
- **Opposition:** palm, pad or side opposition where applicable.
- **Contact patches:** palm, thumb pad/side, individual finger pads, hook region or
  an explicit support surface under the object.
- **Orientation:** which surfaces face the object and which direction they resist.
- **Load assumption:** gravity direction, expected torque, friction/heat uncertainty.

Not every valid hold is a closed power grip. A tray may rest on palms with fingers
stabilizing its edge; a handle may require finger/thumb enclosure; a spoon may use
precision orientation with modest force. Reject a taxonomy label that does not fit
the actual object, scale or task.

## Grip security

A secure animated grip has more than near-zero distance:

1. Contact uses plausible pad or support regions, not nails or the back of the hand.
2. Thumb opposition or another explicit counter-support resists the expected motion.
3. Finger closure matches object diameter and does not cross neighboring digits.
4. Wrist/forearm orientation allows the task without a near-limit wrist pose.
5. The object cannot leave its old support until the required contact roles exist.
6. Contact remains continuous while the hand owns load; relative sliding is bounded
   and motivated rather than a drifting constraint.
7. Release follows new support and ends with a clean withdrawal path.

Without force, friction and material data, call this **plausible support**, not proven
force closure. A reduction in mesh penetration is useful but cannot compensate for
wrong-facing contact, an absent thumb, implausible joints or a missing load path.

## Finger and thumb coordination

- MCP joints contribute flexion and finger spread; PIP/DIP primarily provide the
  finger's flexion chain. Avoid long-axis twisting or lateral hinging as a shortcut.
- Thumb CMC opposition is not the same as applying more thumb curl. Aim the thumb
  contact surface toward the object or opposing fingers.
- Let fingers pre-shape during approach and close as contact becomes available.
  Mechanical simultaneous curl may be acceptable for a stylized distant figure only
  when the silhouette and support role still read.
- Preserve left/right controller independence and inspect the evaluated mesh, not
  only wrist targets or control values.

## Wrist-neutral principle

For forceful, sustained or repetitive handling, begin near a straight wrist/forearm
relationship and distribute orientation through forearm, elbow, shoulder girdle and
body placement. “Neutral” is a working preference, not a ban on expressive wrist
motion. A brief task-specific deviation can be plausible; a loaded near-limit wrist
used to rescue a bad handle/prop position should trigger redesign.

## Two-hand roles

Assign each hand a distinct role when the geometry supports it:

- **primary support + stabilizer** for a bowl or jug body;
- **handle/control + base support** for lifting and pouring;
- **symmetric support** for a tray;
- **drive + guide** for a long tool;
- **operate + brace** for utensils or lids.

Both hands share one object state. Solve their reach, elbows, shoulders, trunk and
feet together. Do not perfect one hand by pulling the other through the object.

## Tools and utensils

- Match handle orientation and diameter to the intended grip and visible hand scale.
- Separate the hand that bears weight from fingers that operate a control, stir,
  cut, pluck or cover holes.
- Tool motion should have a body cause and an object consequence: a spoon meets
  resistance, a lid pivots around its actual rim, a blade/handle follows one rigid
  relationship unless the design says otherwise.
- Heat, sharpness, slippery food and gloves can change grip choice. Record them when
  the story establishes them; do not invent hazards solely to force a new pose.
- Preserve space for fingers and a withdrawal path. A legal final grasp cannot erase
  a handle penetration during approach.

## Minimum evidence for a grasp claim

Inspect palm/back/side views as needed, from separated approach through contact,
load, release and withdrawal. Record intended contact patches, object surfaces,
relative motion, evaluated skin deformation and any hidden/unverified area. Sample
subframes for fast closure or thin handles. Normal-speed readability and close
geometry inspection are separate observations.
