# Evidence, counterexamples and release

## Independent, version-bound tests

- Treat reports, solver names, source comments and previous explanations as claims.
  Use source observations, evaluated geometry and rendered consecutive frames.
- Bind measurements to saved scene and output identities. Recheck changed components
  and affected downstream interactions; a report from a prior export cannot approve
  a new one. Reuse unchanged evidence only with explicit dependency/version support.
- Record exact pairs, geometry, time range, controller states, units and justified
  tolerances. Audit omitted phases/surfaces before trusting green tests.
- Distinguish surface intersections, signed penetration, containment and swept tests.
  Surface no-hits can miss complete containment; discrete checks can miss a thin
  wall crossing between frames. Use subframes/continuous checks where needed.
- Explain proxy approximation: a convex proxy can close a legitimate hole. Do not
  swap proxies or increase tolerance just to obtain a pass.
- Challenge important validators with known-bad cases. A missing hand/support must
  not pass a handoff, a missed mouth must not capture, an upright container must not
  dump, and an illegal underside approach must be caught before legal capture.
  The main entry point links the adversarial case collection for these workflows.

## Scope and perception

- Scrub discontinuities, inspect contact densely and review at delivery speed.
  Finite values, exact targets, still beauty and exit code zero do not certify acting.
- Review the whole story at release, including affected neighboring actors, contact
  effects and audio. During an incremental fix, inspect the changed causal chain
  and preserve justified prior evidence for unrelated parts; this is not a license
  to declare the whole story passed without whole-story coverage.
- If a user disputes approval, reopen that claim and inspect why the check missed
  the fault. Do not defend the result with its earlier green report.
- Use diagnostic close views when the delivery framing hides a contact. Do not move
  the delivery camera to conceal a contradiction. Off-screen preconditions require
  an established story convention, not an excuse.

## Repair and release

- Review alone does not authorize implementation. If implementation is requested,
  repair the mechanism, then pose/timing, then polish; use authored contact, dynamics
  or a hybrid appropriate to the scene rather than migrating for a physics label.
- Use a cheap test of the complete affected interaction before another long render.
  Recheck downstream events and sound. Preserve previous versions and bounded logs.
- Record causal/contact, acting, presentation/material, audio and technical media
  verdicts separately. Readiness is not an average. Failed required categories
  block approval; unobserved required quality remains unverified, not implicitly accepted.
- A final film must cover the agreed story/duration. Inspect the actual output for
  new faults and verify decoding, frame count, duration and audio; provide editable
  source with dependencies. An available excerpt stays labelled an excerpt.
- The installed film-render-delivery skill can execute technical delivery. Invoke
  it only for an authorized render/encoding/delivery task; a quality review can report
  required media checks without launching those operations.
- When an independent reviewer is already available and authorized, give raw evidence
  without coaching the verdict. A vote does not replace inspection.
