# Animation causal staging

Make the audience understand **why this happens now, where it comes from, and
what changes afterward**. Smooth curves, attractive textures and a sound accent
do not by themselves make an event intelligible. This is pre-production design,
not a physics solver or a quality approval.

## Scope first

Keep the user's story, medium, camera and approval boundary. Reuse the current
storyboard and project record; repair the unclear beat rather than inventing a
new film. Distinguish projection content from an advertisement filming it.
Researching advertising craft does not authorize guest hands, camera travel,
logos or sales copy in a projection master.

Choose the **kind of connection** before explaining its mechanism:

- **Physical action:** source, support/contact, feasible movement and resulting
  state. Route anatomy, load and collision details to the existing specialists.
- **In-world fantasy:** an established or clearly introduced trigger and a
  consistent visible rule. Magic is allowed; do not retrospectively excuse
  arbitrary continuity errors as magic.
- **Editorial / graphic transition:** a change of scene or time, not a physical
  object changing identity. State what matches or covers the edit.
- **Deliberate surprise:** preserve the requested surprise. Its source or rule
  may be revealed afterward; do not telegraph every punchline with a mandatory cue.

## Design the smallest useful causal chain

1. Name the beat's purpose and what the viewer must notice. Remove effects that
   only compete with it. Would the action communicate the same thing without them?
2. Track meaningful entities: identity, initial presence/support, entry or emergence
   source, controller, destination/support, persistence/exit. Offscreen, hidden,
   absent and replaced by an edit are different states.
3. Write **trigger → readable cue → action/path → consequence → settle/next cue**.
   Beats may overlap; this is a dependency, not five stop-and-start poses.
   Opening mid-action is valid: make the ongoing source and contact readable.
4. Specify how viewers follow it: silhouette, gaze, direction, contrast, timing
   and sound. A musical accent may motivate an edit, not prove a load transfer.
5. Check available time. Simplify, start in progress or declare editorial ellipsis
   if handling/travel/landing cannot read. Do not disguise teleporting with smoke.
6. Carry resulting states into the next beat: tool stays supported, food stays
   contained, effects decay, partner attention changes for a reason.

For unclear appearances, transformations or transitions read
[transition-patterns.md](references/transition-patterns.md). For attribution or
extending these principles read [research.md](references/research.md); documented
craft and our production inferences are separated there.

## Draw the transition, not just its destination

When visual transition boards are requested, provide images of the outgoing
state, change/coverage point, reveal and result. Add panels if an unseen step
remains ambiguous. A final keyframe and a sentence are not a transition board.

For a requested **full-screen wipe**, full-screen means the complete output
raster, including every corner—not one plate, four islands or the central area.
Show an opaque coverage frame and name the hidden edit interval. A fixed
projection camera can coexist with an explicitly requested editorial cut.
Inspect the board itself: visible table corners contradict “100% covered.”
For a local wipe declare its region; never silently substitute it for full-screen.

Occlusion can conceal an **editorial replacement**. It does not prove an unshown
physical pickup, collision-free path or instantaneous costume change happened.
A shape match expresses a link, not physical causation.

Follow the user's current preparation method. This tabletop series starts with
a green-background one-plate Nano Banana image, existing @ entities and a Kling action brief.
After scene generation, remove/review each plate source's background before four-seat
foreground placement and composition on the selected background;
see the [production loop](../miniature-film-director/references/collaborative-production-loop.md).
Storyboards do not require a Blender previs. Use images and observable action
instructions unless a requested or specifically needed 3D check changes that choice.
Read [storyboard-previs.md](references/storyboard-previs.md) only for selected mesh
previs work; preserve its requested interval or whole duration.

## Hand off observable instructions

Use [causal-beat-packet.md](assets/causal-beat-packet.md) only when a paragraph is
insufficient. Keep stable beat/entity IDs in the existing project record.
Separate scene events from compositing events and proposals from observations.

- [character-performance-animation](../character-performance-animation/SKILL.md)
  owns intention, thought, gaze, timing texture and acting within the beat.
- [visual-video](../visual-video/SKILL.md) owns shot/edit language;
  [visual-image](../visual-image/SKILL.md) owns image direction and references.
- [human-motion-biomechanics](../human-motion-biomechanics/SKILL.md),
  [blender-contact-animation](../blender-contact-animation/SKILL.md) and
  [food-vfx-director](../food-vfx-director/SKILL.md) supply relevant physical details.
- [animation-causality-review](../animation-causality-review/SKILL.md) checks the
  actual candidate; a good plan is never an observed pass.

Do not require extra renders, research or approvals for every shot. Stop at the
requested artifact and respect explicit storyboard confirmation gates. This skill
does not authorize paid calls, retries or private uploads. Use
[forward-cases.md](references/forward-cases.md) when changing this skill.
