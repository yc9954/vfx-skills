# Pose, silhouette and line of action

A key pose is a storytelling statement, not a sampled point on a trajectory. It
should remain intelligible in the delivery camera, at intended scale, with face
detail temporarily hidden.

## Strong key-pose test

For each planned key pose ask:

1. What thought, decision, contact or result changed here?
2. Where does the viewer look first, second and third?
3. Is the action legible as a filled silhouette?
4. Is the prop/body relationship unambiguous?
5. Does the pose have a clear line of action without losing balance or contact?
6. Is the pose specific to this character rather than a generic emotion icon?

If two adjacent poses say the same thing, combine them or make the contrast useful.

## Silhouette and readability

- Separate limbs, hands, face contour and prop openings enough to read from the
  approved camera. Do not solve hidden contact by changing a locked master camera.
- Preserve clean negative spaces. Tangencies between wrist, torso, jug handle and
  partner can erase ownership even when the 3D contact is correct.
- Keep the primary action in the strongest shape; secondary gestures should sit
  inside or beside it without creating a competing contour.
- Check front, profile and camera view during blocking, but optimize final clarity
  for the actual delivery view. Diagnostic views verify geometry, not staging.

## Line of action and contrapposto

Use a dominant directional flow through head, ribcage, pelvis and supporting leg.
Curves can show openness, strain, hesitation, pride or softness; straight/angled
lines can show resolve, stiffness or alarm. Do not paste an S-curve onto every pose.

Contrapposto is a relationship among support, pelvis, ribcage and shoulders, not a
fashion pose. Let load and support create useful counter-angles. Under a jug, the
supporting structure must still explain the weight even if the silhouette is pushed.

## Asymmetry

Perfect mirror poses often feel diagrammatic. Use purposeful asymmetry in shoulder
height, elbow bend, hand role, face tension, stance or gaze to show role and thought.
Asymmetry must follow the action: one hand controls while the other supports; one
brow leads a doubtful reaction; one foot accepts more load. Random offsets or broken
eye convergence are defects, not charm.

## Arcs and pose transitions

- Track head, hands, elbows, prop centre and gaze target through motivated arcs.
- A natural joint path is often curved, but contact may require a constrained line
  or pivot. Preserve the cause rather than forcing circular motion.
- Use breakdowns to define which body part leads, where the path bends, when the
  silhouette opens and how the contact survives.
- Check the pose immediately before and after maximum extension; legal endpoints
  can conceal a pop, elbow flip or tangent collapse between them.

## Staging and attention control

Build one primary read per moment. Direct attention with orientation, gaze, stillness,
contrast, local light and partner reaction. In a locked Projection Master, these are
the principal tools; do not introduce promo-style zooms, cuts or reframing.

For ensembles, offset reactions so the audience can follow the cause. A partner may
notice first, then the worker reacts, then a tertiary character confirms the result.
If synchronized action is the story, keep the sync and vary personality in recovery,
not by randomly breaking the shared event.
