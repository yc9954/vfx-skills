# Ingredient identity through the story

Use when a food model is anatomically confused, an edible part has the wrong scale,
or a transformation breaks the action. Keep declared fantasy, biological reference
and the requested cooking story distinct. Do not turn research into a different plot.

## Identify shell, animal and edible part

| Subject | Required design distinction |
|---|---|
| Scallop / another bivalve | Identify the species; model paired valves, hinge and intended interior. A scallop adductor is an edible muscle, not a loose shell or the entire animal. Do not transfer one species' proportions to every clam. |
| Abalone | Use a single shell over a broad muscular foot against the rock. Preserve shell relief/volume and the underside attachment; it is not a hinged two-valve scallop or a flat sticker. |
| Removed ingredient | Name the actual meat/part that separates, where it was attached, the tool/contact causing release and the remaining shell/body. A shell cannot stand in for the requested meat. |

Use the selected species and the user's visual reference for proportions. Do not
invent a universal meat-to-shell ratio. Compare the flesh against the interior,
utensil, character and final plate at delivery resolution; an enormous decorative
shell with a tiny arbitrary pellet may fail the requested read even if a prop fits.

For a fantasy scene in which a character enters a giant closing clam, preserve
readable cavity volume, hinge path, contact/support and safe story blocking. An
anatomical study does not by itself establish room for the actor or a collision-free
closure. Give these surfaces and intervals to the Blender/contact specialist.

## Enter at the right episode

Write `initial location/visibility | cue | entry path | attachment/support | action |
remaining material | final location`. If an abalone appears later on an upper-left
rock, do not initialize it visibly in the opening. Its entry must carry the rock
attachment consistently; avoid an independent hover above the rock or a visibility
pop. Define whether the rock moves, the camera reveals it or a declared transition
introduces it. Do not invent camera travel in a locked projection master.

## Lift and cook as one continuous event

- Track one ingredient identity through grip/capture, rise, transformation and
  placement. For a giant shrimp becoming cooked while lifted, preserve the gripped
  location, readable body/appendages and continuous trajectory through the change.
- Separate size, shape, pose, color, opacity and cooked-material channels. If raw
  and cooked meshes are exchanged, align their contact anchors and compatible
  silhouettes. Avoid empty frames, doubled overlapping bodies, sudden size jumps
  and a cooked replacement that appears elsewhere on the plate.
- Motivate timing with the story's heat/effect cue or explicit magical convention.
  A fantasy transformation may be exaggerated; it still needs a readable cause,
  retained identity and a stable support. Inspect onset, middle, end and landing.
- Keep final plating credible: food rests on the actual dish, utensils have room,
  pieces retain intended identity/count and materials have scene-scale wetness or
  scattering. Do not enlarge the dish or merge the food into one glossy mass to
  repair a placement problem.

## Fire and water belong to the event

Name the emitter and visible flame shape, direction, reach, onset and decay. An
exaggerated dragon-mouth flame can have a broad source-connected core and changing
tapered tongues; a point light alone has no flame body. Inspect depth/occlusion,
light response on nearby objects and actor clearance over the complete event.
Do not claim combustion simulation from an authored emissive effect.

Use wet edges, ripples, wake/foam and drainage where food or hull actually meets
water. Material brightness and bubbles do not compensate for missing depth or a
shell hovering above the surface. Route overall look integration to `visual-image`.

## Research boundary

Biological sources checked 2026-11-19:

- [NOAA white abalone](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/white-abalone):
  muscular foot adheres to hard surfaces; shell protects the animal.
- [NOAA pinto abalone](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/pinto-abalone/science):
  one shell and a muscular foot used for movement and attachment.
- [NOAA Atlantic sea scallop](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/atlantic-sea-scallop/seafood):
  paired shell action driven by the adductor muscle.

These identify structures and behavior, not a verified reconstruction of the
user's species, cooking technique or fantasy scale. Validate the actual model and
action against their references; do not claim multi-view generation without its inputs.
