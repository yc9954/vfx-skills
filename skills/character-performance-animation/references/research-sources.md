# Research sources and application limits

Checked 2026-09-30. This is a selective production bibliography, not a copied
animation manual. The skill paraphrases general principles and does not reproduce
characters, films, house style, proprietary tools or long passages.

## Public studio process and education

| Source | What it supports here | Application limit |
|---|---|---|
| [Walt Disney Animation Studios — Animation](https://www.disneyanimation.com/process/animation/) | Character animation combines timing, staging, anticipation, follow-through, secondary action, anatomy, weight and appeal to create emotionally driven performance. | High-level studio process, not a shot recipe or permission to imitate a film style. |
| [Walt Disney Animation Studios — Hand-Drawn Animation](https://www.disneyanimation.com/process/hand-drawn-animation/) | Strong poses and facial expressions communicate thought/feeling; rough planning precedes cleanup; overlapping action and personality remain central. | Describes hand-drawn production; transfer principles, not drawing-specific mechanics. |
| [Walt Disney Animation Studios — Layout](https://www.disneyanimation.com/process/layout/) | Staging, rough blocking, framing, emotion and timing are established through iteration before shot polish. | Camera freedom described there does not override a locked Projection Master. |
| [Walt Disney Animation Studios — Technical Animation](https://www.disneyanimation.com/process/technical-animation/) | Drag and overlap in cloth, hair, skin and accessories are art-directed to remain intentional and physically believable. | Secondary simulation supports performance; it does not author the acting objective or prove physical accuracy. |
| [The Science Behind Pixar — Animation](https://sciencebehindpixar.org/pipeline/animation) | “Animation is acting”: important poses/keyframes express the scene; interpolation curves shape motion and emotion between them. | Museum/Pixar educational overview; it does not specify one universal spline or timing curve. |
| [Pixar in a Box — Animation](https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar/animate) | Straight-ahead work, interpolation, Bézier curves and squash/stretch demonstrate that mathematical in-betweens need artistic control. | Ball exercises teach fundamentals, not full facial or prop acting. |
| [Pixar in a Box — Art of Storytelling](https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar/storytelling) | Character wants/needs, visual language, major/minor beats, storyreels and feedback connect performance to narrative intent. | Educational material; adapt to the user's story rather than importing Pixar story formulas. |
| [The Science Behind Pixar — Ask a Pixar Scientist](https://sciencebehindpixar.org/ask-a-pixar-scientist) | A walk's hard part is attitude/acting; animators identify key poses and use splines for in-betweens. | Informal educational Q&A, useful for workflow distinction rather than empirical thresholds. |

## Traditional principles and production research

| Source | What it supports here | Application limit |
|---|---|---|
| [Lasseter, “Principles of Traditional Animation Applied to 3D Computer Animation,” SIGGRAPH 1987](https://doi.org/10.1145/37401.37407) | Classical principles transfer to 3D: squash/stretch, timing, anticipation, staging, follow-through/overlap, straight-ahead/pose-to-pose, slow in/out, arcs, exaggeration, secondary action and appeal. | Historical synthesis. Apply as decision tools, not a compliance checklist or branded style. |
| Historical lead: Pixar, “Articulating the Appeal” (old graphics.pixar.com/library/ArticulatingAppeal/paper.pdf) | **Not currently verified as a paper source.** On 2026-09-30 the URL redirects to the generic technology libraries page. | Do not cite the redirected page as support for paper-specific claims. Locate and read the actual publication before reinstating it; use the verified studio process sources above for broad pose/readability guidance. |
| [Pixar, “Acting with Contact in Ratatouille”](https://graphics.pixar.com/library/CartoonCollision/paper.pdf) | Contact can be broad or subtle, art-directed and silhouette-conscious; compelling cartoon contact need not be a literal dynamics solve. | Describes a contact-deformation tool. It does not validate a new scene's grip, force, safety or load path. |
| [Pixar, “Art and Technology at Pixar”](https://graphics.pixar.com/library/sigAsia2018Course/paper.pdf) | Broad clear gestures remain central; simulation should enhance hand-keyed poses, stylized timing and directed secondary motion. | Multi-production technical course. Tool examples are evidence of workflow, not required software. |
| [Pixar, “Inside Out 2: Character Rig Challenges and Techniques”](https://graphics.pixar.com/library/InsideOut2Rig/index.html) | Stylized eye geometry may need gaze correction; layered controls support broad mouth/face silhouette and detailed expression polish. | Character-specific rig case. Do not copy character design or assume the same controls exist. |
| [Pixar, “Eyes Without a Face”](https://graphics.pixar.com/library/DFF/paper.pdf) | Graphic facial features can be decoupled and pushed, but still require coherent visual connection and design-aware expressive range. | A specialized feature pipeline, not a general instruction to detach features. |
| [Pixar, “Elemental Characters: Making Characters Out of Thin Air”](https://graphics.pixar.com/library/ElementalAir/paper.pdf) | Procedural/simulated character material must preserve readable hand and facial performance. | Elemental production case; applies as preservation principle, not material recipe. |

## Gaze, blinks and facial performance research

| Source | What it supports here | Application limit |
|---|---|---|
| [Disney Research, “Realistic and Interactive Robot Gaze”](https://la.disneyresearch.com/publication/realistic-and-interactive-robot-gaze/) | Believable gaze benefits from saliency, attention selection, habituation, saccades and layered motions with different response bandwidths. | Interactive animatronics research; use as gaze-design evidence, not fixed film timing or human diagnosis. |
| [Disney Research, “Modeling and Animating Eye Blinks”](https://la.disneyresearch.com/publication/modeling-and-animating-eye-blinks/) | Natural blinks show spatial and temporal asymmetry; data-driven full closures were perceived as more natural than simple symmetric textbook curves in the tested characters. | Two-character study. Preserve stylization and do not universalize one human blink model. |
| [Disney Research, “PAPILLON: Expressive Eyes for Interactive Characters”](https://la.disneyresearch.com/publication/papillon-expressive-eyes-for-interactive-characters/) | Eyes are a major channel for face-to-face expression and deserve explicit design/control. | Hardware/display work; supports importance, not a complete film-acting method. |
| [Disney Research, “Content Retargeting Using Parameter-Parallel Facial Layers”](https://la.disneyresearch.com/publication/content-retargeting-using-parameter-parallel-facial-layers/) | Emotion, speech and blink can be treated as distinct but composable facial layers across structurally different characters. | Retargeting research; layers still need artistic coordination and character-specific review. |
| [Disney Research, “Facial Performance Enhancement Using Dynamic Shape Space Analysis”](https://la.disneyresearch.com/publication/facial-performance-enhancement-using-dynamic-shape-space-analysis/) | Compelling facial work contains individual-specific spatial and temporal nuance often missing from low-resolution performance. | Enhancement/capture method; supports specificity and micro-timing, not automatic quality from added detail. |
| [Disney Research, “Enriching Facial Blendshape Rigs with Physical Simulation”](https://la.disneyresearch.com/publication/enriching-facial-blendshape-rigs-with-physical-simulation/) | Body/head dynamics and facial motion must remain coherent; secondary tissue response should preserve the authored face performance. | Physical face simulation research; not required for stylized miniature rigs. |

## How evidence is used

- Studio process and classical principles justify the workflow and vocabulary.
- Technical papers support bounded claims about silhouette, gaze, face and secondary
  motion; they are not evidence that a local rig or video model has those capabilities.
- Biomechanics claims come from `human-motion-biomechanics`, not from animation craft
  papers. Provider features come from current tool/schema evidence in `visual-video`
  and `blender-seedance-handoff`.
- The SURA Tarakjuk beat sheet is an original production synthesis derived from the
  current scene requirements and these general principles. It is not an excerpt from
  or imitation of a Disney/Pixar scene.
