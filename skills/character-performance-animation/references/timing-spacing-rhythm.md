# Timing, spacing and rhythm

Timing is the number and placement of moments; spacing is the visible distance
covered between them. Together they communicate thought speed, force, weight,
confidence and emphasis. Avoid solving every transition with the same Bézier ease.

## Phrase before frame counts

Build a phrase from meaningful events:

`prepare → commit → impact/transfer → register → recover`

Not every phrase needs all five. Choose a dominant accent and arrange smaller
events around it. Then assign frames to fit the current FPS and duration. A timing
label is a design target, not proof that a video model will obey a frame boundary.

## Spacing patterns

- **Compressed spacing** near a pose creates a hold or soft arrival.
- **Widening spacing** reads as acceleration or loss of restraint.
- **Narrowing spacing** reads as deceleration, caution or control.
- **A sharp spacing change** can create an accent, recoil or comic take; it needs a
  motivated before/after state.
- **Nearly even spacing** can communicate machinery, discipline or deliberate
  steadiness. Use it as character choice, not the default spline result.

Inspect the screen path. Equal graph intervals can still create uneven visible
motion under perspective, rotation or parent transforms.

## Holds and moving holds

A hold protects an idea long enough to read. It is not a frozen duplicate frame.
Specify:

- what remains stable: the key silhouette, grip, eye target, balance or prop state;
- what remains alive: breathing, a small eye refocus, finger pressure, cloth settling,
  a blink or a tiny weight adjustment;
- what must not drift: contact patches, registered camera anchors, prop scale or the
  emotion's direction.

Do not animate all available channels during a hold. One or two coherent signs of
life are usually stronger than continuous noise.

## Rhythm, tempo and timing texture

- **Tempo** is the broad rate of activity. **Rhythm** is the pattern of starts,
  stops and accents. **Phrasing** groups events into meaningful units.
- Vary beat lengths according to thought and effort. A cautious character may take
  longer to commit and recover quickly; an impulsive one may commit early and need
  a larger correction.
- Use timing texture: a brisk eye dart, slower head follow, measured torso turn and
  later sleeve settle can coexist within one action.
- Preserve pauses before decisions and after consequences. Removing every pause
  to “improve flow” often erases acting.
- Repetition gains life through controlled variation in amplitude, phase, focus or
  recovery. Random offsets are not rhythm.

## Comedy and emphasis

Comedy often depends on setup, readable expectation, a withheld beat, reversal and
an economical reaction. Do not force a gag into a sincere scene.

- Establish the normal pattern before breaking it.
- Use contrast: fast/slow, broad/still, confident/uncertain, synchronized/offset.
- Let the audience register the cause before the reaction unless surprise requires
  an intentional reaction-before-explanation structure.
- A pause is active when gaze, tension and expectation remain directed.
- Accents may snap, but the body still needs a plausible support and subsequent
  absorption or settle.

## Overshoot, recoil and settle

An overshoot crosses the intended endpoint because of momentum, force or emotional
commitment. A recoil responds to impact or a sudden stop. A settle removes residual
energy through one or more diminishing, non-identical responses.

Name the chain: “jug reaches upright; forearms stop; shoulders recover slightly;
sleeves and fingertips settle last.” Avoid symmetrical ping-pong oscillation or an
endless damped sine wave. Heavy, soft, rigid and nervous characters settle differently.

## Review questions

- Does each pause protect a readable thought or state?
- Are important events separated enough to be perceived at delivery scale?
- Do all body parts start and stop together without reason?
- Are accents flattened by smoothing or multiplied until none is dominant?
- Does the timing belong to this personality, load and relationship?
