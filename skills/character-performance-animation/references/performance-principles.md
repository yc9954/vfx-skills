# Performance principles: from intention to polish

Use this reference when an action is technically complete but plays as a list of
operations. The goal is not constant movement. The goal is a continuous chain of
thought, choice, action, consequence and recovery.

## The working stack

Design from top to bottom, then test from bottom to top:

1. **Objective and relationship** — what the character wants now, from whom or
   from what, and what they are monitoring.
2. **Thought/emotional beats** — changes of attention, certainty, effort, surprise,
   relief or resolve. A beat exists because the inner state changes, not because a
   new body part moves.
3. **Key poses and contact states** — the clearest physical statements of those
   beats, including prop ownership and partner relationship.
4. **Timing and transition design** — spacing, holds, accents, contrast, arcs,
   anticipation, overshoot and settle.
5. **Performance detail** — gaze, face, hands, breath and secondary/tertiary motion.
6. **Polish** — curve continuity, deformation and cleanup that preserve the above.

If polish weakens a pose, erases an accent or makes all parts arrive together,
return to blocking instead of adding more keys.

## Pose-to-pose, straight-ahead and spline work

- Use **pose-to-pose** for acting, dialogue, prop contact, shared glances and exact
  support changes. Establish story poses, breakdowns and contact landmarks before
  interpolation. Check them in stepped playback and as silhouettes.
- Use **straight-ahead** exploration for elastic follow-through, cloth, hair, tails,
  a loose recoil or a spontaneous flourish. Anchor its start, peak and end so it
  cannot change identity, contact, duration or intended result.
- A production shot may combine them: pose-to-pose for body/face/prop ownership,
  straight-ahead for selected connective arcs, then spline polishing.
- During spline polish inspect spacing in screen space as well as graph shape.
  Delete keys that produce mush. Preserve deliberate corners, pauses and accents;
  mathematically smooth is not automatically alive.

## Classical principles as decisions

| Principle | Performance decision | Common mechanical failure |
|---|---|---|
| Squash and stretch | Show force, flexibility or emotional compression while preserving designed mass/identity | Scaling every beat or stretching a rigid prop |
| Anticipation | Reveal preparation, attention or intent before the main event | A decorative wind-up unrelated to force or thought |
| Staging | Make one primary idea readable through pose, gaze, silhouette and contrast | Every actor and limb demands attention at once |
| Follow-through/overlap | Let connected masses and decisions stop on related but distinct timings | Uniform lag pasted onto every control |
| Straight-ahead/pose-to-pose | Choose exploration or planned clarity by layer | Letting splines invent contact and acting |
| Slow in/out and spacing | Shape perceived weight, effort, confidence and emphasis | Same ease curve and duration on every transition |
| Arcs | Give joints, gaze and props motivated curved paths | Generic circular arcs that miss contact or intent |
| Secondary action | Reinforce the primary idea without stealing the read | Random hand, bounce or cloth motion |
| Timing | Define weight, thought speed, status and comedy | Treating timing as only total duration |
| Exaggeration | Clarify the essence of a pose, rhythm or reaction | More amplitude everywhere, regardless of design |
| Solid posing | Preserve balance, perspective, volume and spatial relation | Attractive 2D silhouette with impossible support |
| Appeal | Make the character specific, readable and engaging | Equating appeal with large eyes, smiles or constant bounce |

## Physical plausibility floor

The biomechanics contract fixes required supports, contacts, working-range facts,
load transfer and phase order. Performance may exaggerate pose contrast, timing,
facial response, line of action and brief deformation only where character design
and the production method permit it.

Never improve appeal by breaking grip, changing support foot, stretching a limb,
making a rigid jug rubbery or removing the effort caused by the load. A one- or
two-frame accent can be stylized, but it still needs a readable cause and a return
to a valid state. Mark untested dynamics as art-directed, not physically proven.

## Prop interaction is acting

Treat the prop as a scene partner. The character sees it, judges it, approaches,
establishes contact, feels its resistance/weight, changes strategy, checks the
result and releases only after new support exists. The hand pose, gaze and torso
must tell the same ownership story. Prop timing can reveal care, haste, expertise,
status, fear or affection without adding unrelated gestures.

## Minimum deliverable

For each beat record:

`time/phrase | thought/emotion change | primary action | key pose/line | gaze/face |
support/contact invariant | secondary/tertiary motion | end state | review cue`

Do not claim a performance is successful until the produced candidate is watched
at normal speed and readable scale.
