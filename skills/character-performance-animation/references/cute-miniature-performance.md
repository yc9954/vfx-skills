# Restrained appeal for miniature performance

This reference serves small tabletop characters and the SURA projection workflow.
It is project guidance, not a universal definition of cute.

## Appeal without caricature shortcuts

Build cuteness from readable intention, restrained asymmetry, timing contrast,
specific relationships and economical recovery. Do not default to enlarged eyes,
infant proportions, constant bounce, repeated foot taps, mirrored smiles or random
hand flutter. Preserve the approved character design.

At small scale prioritize:

1. clear whole-body silhouette and head direction;
2. one readable action/attention target per moment;
3. purposeful pauses and contrast;
4. visible partner/prop relationship;
5. facial microdetail only when resolution supports it.

## Projection Master attention

Keep the registered camera, crop, plate anchors, focus behavior and mapping fixed.
Guide attention with character blocking, gaze, pose contrast, stillness, local light/
material contrast and staggered reactions. A new performance beat is not a camera cut.
Promo/cinematic coverage may use moves and edits only when it is a separately named
deliverable; never import that freedom into the projection master.

## SURA Tarakjuk milk-pour regression

This is a challenge case, not a universal pouring recipe. Preserve the actual guide's
left/right grip roles, support geometry, load assumption, duration and camera. The
biomechanics regression remains authoritative for ROM, weight shift and contact.

### Mechanical shorthand to replace

`grip → lift → tilt → pour → upright → return`

This sequence is causally necessary but performs only state changes. Do not remove
it; layer a continuous acting phrase around it.

### Organic performance beat sheet

This table illustrates a **two-character, moderately effortful** variant. For a
solo or expert performer, omit partner beats and replace unnecessary overshoot or
recoil with controlled arrival. Instantiate a scene-specific subset before use;
never turn this example into mandatory acting or add nonexistent face controls.

| Phrase | Thought and attention | Body/prop performance | Face, partner and overlap | Invariant |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Notice/prepare | Maid sees jug, checks bowl, gives partner a brief shared glance | torso organizes toward task; line of action inclines; hands pre-shape | partner receives glance while stirring continues quietly | camera, prop support, partner task |
| Anticipate contact | eyes return to grip regions; breath gathers | small weight shift precedes reach; shoulders/arms approach on arcs | restrained lid/brow focus; sleeves follow arms | jug does not move before grip |
| Acquire/commit | attention locks on contact and intended path | both contacts establish; fingers close; body accepts load | partner reaction is slightly offset, not simultaneous | exact grip roles and support transfer |
| Lift | character registers weight, then continues | feet/pelvis/torso join; jug rises close; tiny lift overshoot followed by control settle | brief effort in lids/cheeks/jaw; sleeves and body lag differently | working range, continuous grip |
| Move/tilt/pour | gaze alternates sparingly between rim/receiver; intention stays precise | line of action extends; controlled tilt with no stop-start segmentation | free facial/shoulder detail supports focus; partner's stir rhythm softens or pauses after cause | emitter/receiver and contact fixed |
| Maintain moving hold | pour pose and eye target stay stable | breathing and tiny balance correction keep pose alive | fingers maintain pressure; sleeves decay; partner watches, then resumes an offset stir | no drift in camera, feet, grip or stream interface |
| Prepare end | eyes find return support before the jug moves; head follows | torso begins recovery while hands control upright path | partner anticipates completion rather than snapping with it | support destination remains visible |
| Upright/recoil | jug reaches upright with controlled deceleration | small recoil/overshoot reflects momentum; forearms stop before shoulders/sleeves | mouth/jaw tension releases; partner registers result | no float or grip redesign |
| Re-support/release | maid verifies the jug is supported | new support accepts load, fingers open, hands withdraw | relief exhale and small asymmetric smile; shared glance only if story needs it | support before release |
| Staggered settle | attention moves to next task after result is clear | prop still; torso/shoulders, hands, sleeves and breath settle on nearby distinct beats | partner's stir rhythm returns; neither character loops idle noise | explicit end pose, support and velocity |

Durations are chosen from the actual scene. Do not give every row equal time or turn
the table into ten visible stops. Several beats should overlap: eyes may find the
return support while the final pour finishes; partner reaction may begin while the
maid holds the main action; relief may start in breath before the smile is complete.

## Observable Seedance/Higgsfield direction block

Use as a provider-neutral craft example, not executable schema:

The example below assumes two actors. Rewrite its inventory and performance to
the actual scene contract; it is not a ready-to-submit prompt for a solo guide.

```text
The registered projection camera and plate geometry remain completely fixed. The
maid first looks to the bowl, trades one brief confirming glance with her partner,
then returns her eyes to the jug. Her weight shifts toward the vessel before either
hand closes around the approved grip regions. Only after both grips are secure does
the jug leave its support.

She lifts close to her body; the jug rises a touch past its working height and settles
under control. Her eyelids tighten slightly with effort while shoulders and torso join
the lift. As she tilts, her forearms lead; her sleeves and fingertips follow a fraction
later and settle at different moments without sliding the grip. The partner notices
after the lift begins and eases the stirring rhythm rather than mirroring her timing.

During the pour the main silhouette and eye target stay stable, but breath, tiny finger
pressure and the last sleeve fold remain alive. Before the stream ends, her eyes find
the return support and her head follows. She rotates the jug upright with a small recoil,
sets it down before releasing, exhales, and allows a restrained asymmetric smile. Hands,
shoulders, sleeves and partner stirring return to rest on staggered beats. Preserve the
exact camera, identities, two-handed grip, support feet, jug opening and receiver.
```

Provider/version/mode settings still require current verification in `visual-video`
and `blender-seedance-handoff`. This block designs observable performance only.
