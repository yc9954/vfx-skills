# Personality and emotional beats

Personality is a pattern of choices under the scene's conditions, not a permanent
adjective or a reusable motion clip. Define how this character notices, decides,
commits, handles error, relates to others and recovers.

## Character-performance card

Record only useful distinctions:

`want now | attention habit | decision speed | effort strategy | spatial/status habit |
gesture economy | gaze/social style | error response | recovery style | forbidden cliché`

Examples are dimensions, not presets: direct vs circling approach; early vs delayed
commitment; broad vs economical gesture; open vs guarded posture; partner-seeking vs
self-contained check; quick correction vs embarrassed pause.

## Thought before action

Before a meaningful action identify the thought trigger. It may be a glance, sound,
partner cue, tactile discovery or remembered plan. Show only enough to make intention
readable. Familiar routine may use a tiny eye check; uncertainty may need a longer
look, breath suspension and hand hover.

Map each emotional change to physical evidence:

| Inner event | Possible observable evidence |
|---|---|
| notices | eye dart or head orientation; action pauses or redirects |
| evaluates | dwell, narrowed/lifted lids, hand hover, breath change |
| decides | focus locks, line of action organizes, weight prepares |
| commits | spacing opens, contact/load changes, gesture becomes directional |
| registers result | gaze moves to consequence; face/body absorb outcome |
| recovers | exhale, posture releases, shared glance or next-target lead |

Choose evidence that fits this character and shot; do not use every row mechanically.

## Reaction before action

A visible reaction can precede a corrective action: the character feels unexpected
weight, tightens grip, then recruits the torso; hears a partner, looks, then redirects.
This establishes cause and makes the next action intentional. The delay must not be
so long that contact or safety becomes implausible.

## Hands as performance

Hand gestures express precision, status, care and uncertainty, but grip obligations
come first. Use free fingers, pressure changes, approach shape and release style where
the task allows. Do not detach a support hand to gesture during a loaded phase. A
hesitant hover, expert pre-shape or careful fingertip check can be more specific than
a broad wave.

## Partners and ensemble rhythm

- Give every partner an attention state even when still: watching, anticipating,
  missing the cue, helping, judging or continuing another task.
- Offset reactions from the cause so the exchange reads. Do not make everyone snap
  to the same clock unless synchronized protocol is intentional.
- Shared glances are relational events. Define who initiates, who receives, what is
  understood and who breaks the look.
- Secondary performers support the scene's attention path. Their motion can continue
  with a quieter rhythm, pause at the primary accent and resume with a changed state.
- For cooperative contact, personality variation cannot change the shared pickup,
  handoff or load-transfer event.

## Contrast, reversals and accents

Use contrast to reveal character: poised preparation followed by a tiny unexpected
correction; brisk routine interrupted by a careful check; restrained body with an
expressive eye movement. A reversal changes intention, direction, status or emotion,
not merely motion direction. Give the old state enough time to establish before the
new one replaces it.

## Prop interaction as character

The same prop action should differ by person:

- an expert pre-shapes the hands, looks ahead and makes small corrections;
- a novice watches the contact closely, over-prepares and registers success;
- a proud character suppresses effort until a brief recovery leaks through;
- a caring character protects the receiver and checks the partner before release.

These are hypotheses to adapt, not stereotypes. Cultural/historical gesture and
role-specific etiquette require appropriate references; generic animation craft is
not evidence of authenticity.
